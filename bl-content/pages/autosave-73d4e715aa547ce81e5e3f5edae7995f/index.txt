<p>A good colour palette brings a website to life. To make it work for everyone, though, the colours also need enough contrast with each other, so that text, buttons and icons are easy to see and read.</p>
<p>Most contrast checkers test one colour against another, which is fine until you have eight brand colours, 56 possible combinations and the patience for roughly none of them.</p>
<p>So I built a tool that checks the lot.</p>
<h2>What it does</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Checks every pair at once.</strong> Add as many colours as you like. It starts you off with one random colour, just to break the ice.</li>
<li><strong>Tests three things for each pair:</strong> normal text, large text, and UI components and graphics, against WCAG 2.2 AA or AAA.</li>
<li><strong>Shows results your way.</strong> Cards, one per background colour, or a table with every combination laid out like a seating plan.</li>
<li><strong>Names your colours.</strong> Names are added automatically, and you can change them whenever you like..</li>
<li><strong>Shares and exports.</strong> Copy a link to your palette, or copy it as CSS custom properties, ready to drop into your project.</li>
<li><strong>Light or dark, online or off.</strong> It follows your system theme, and you can install it and use it without a connection.</li>
</ul>
<h2>It practises what it preaches</h2>
<p>The tool is fully keyboard operable, screen readers hear what changes, and results never rely on colour alone. Every pass and fail comes with a tick or a cross and the actual word, because a contrast checker that only speaks in red and green would be a bit embarrassing.</p>
<h2>A gentle reminder</h2>
<p>Good contrast won't make a website accessible on its own, but poor contrast will certainly make it harder to use. Think of this as one brushstroke in a much bigger picture.</p>
